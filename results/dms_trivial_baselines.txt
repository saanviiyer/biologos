Two baselines, because they answer different questions.

  ZERO-SHOT, the like-for-like comparison to a likelihood. BLOSUM62 alone,
  no fitting and no model of any kind, reaches +0.228, which is 49% of ESM-2's
  +0.465 and positive on 24/25 assays. ESM-2 minus BLOSUM62, paired: +0.237 [+0.178, +0.296], higher in 23/25.

  FITTED, five chemistry scalars with weights learned on other protein
  clusters and never on the test protein: +0.286, 61% of ESM-2. ESM-2 minus
  that, paired: +0.179 [+0.120, +0.239], higher in 23/25.

So roughly half to two thirds of the DMS signal usually credited to a protein
language model is generic amino-acid chemistry that a lookup table already
has. ESM-2's genuinely learned margin over the fitted chemistry baseline is
+0.179, not +0.465.

The bar a genomic model has to clear is therefore +0.228 zero-shot, not +0.465.
NT-v2 is at -0.013 and does not clear it: BLOSUM62 minus NT-v2, paired, +0.241 [+0.200, +0.283], higher in 24/25.
Moving the bar does not rescue the genomic rung; it relocates the headline. The
honest statement of section 18 is that a genomic LM scores below a substitution
matrix, which is a stronger and cheaper claim than scoring below ESM-2.
