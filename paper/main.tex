\begin{abstract}
A substitution matrix with no learned parameters reaches 49\% of a
650M-parameter protein language model at variant-effect prediction, and the
strongest genomic model we test does not separate from it. What a transfer claim
is measured against therefore decides it. DNA and protein are two encodings of one
molecule related by an exactly known map, which makes biological sequence models a
sharp setting for a question the rest of machine learning can only ask loosely: when a model trained on one
representation appears to know something about another, is that transfer, or an
artifact of how the question was posed? We give four controls that decide it ---
a noise ceiling read off the model's own violation of an invariance it should
respect, manipulations that destroy the representation map while preserving
surface statistics, composition-matched context twins, and trivial baselines
under correlated-unit accounting. Each is stated against a precondition rather
than against biology, though we demonstrate them only in biology. Applying them
overturns four claims, three of them our own: a genomic representation read as
weak transfer at $\rho=+0.141$ scores $+0.143$ once its reading frame is
destroyed, and a context dose-response is reproduced by flanks carrying no
genomic information. Across 25 deep mutational scanning assays in 9 organisms no
Nucleotide Transformer size carries protein-fitness signal, and Evo~2 7B reaches
$+0.266$ but does not separate from BLOSUM62 at $+0.228$. Three explanations
separable from released checkpoints at matched architecture --- codon-aligned
tokenization, within-species corpus diversity, and corpus breadth over 850
species --- each leave the result short of that bar. On the third our two
interval estimators disagree, one excluding zero by $0.0011$ and one not, and we
report the disagreement rather than choosing the convenient side of it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Claims that a model trained on one representation has learned something about
another are common and consequential. A genomic language model that scored protein
fitness would let practitioners rank variants without a protein model, and would be
evidence that the model had internalised the genetic code and not merely nucleotide
statistics. Similar claims are made for multimodal encoders, for code models
reasoning about natural language, for cross-lingual transfer, where whether a
multilingual model shares structure or merely shares vocabulary was contested for
several years, and for any system said to have acquired a capability it was not
trained on.

These claims are hard to check because the representations are not independent.
Under any encoding rule the codon determines the amino acid, so the nucleotides at
a substituted site already \emph{are} residue identity. A probe that recovers
residue identity from DNA has not demonstrated a protein-level representation; it
has read a lookup table. The difficulty is general. Whenever representation $A$
determines property $P$ of modality $B$ by a known map, a measurement that claims
$A$ encodes $P$ ``at the level of $B$'' must rule out the map itself.

We ask whether the apparent transfer survives being asked properly, and find that
most of it does not. Four checks decide the cases here: a noise ceiling read off
the model's own violation of an invariance it should respect, manipulations that
destroy the representation map while preserving surface statistics,
composition-matched context twins, and trivial baselines under correlated-unit
accounting. Each is stated against a precondition rather than against biology, so
each should carry wherever a known map relates two representations. We have not
shown that it does: every number here comes from biological sequence models on one
benchmark. The Limitations section says what that leaves open.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep0pt
\item \textbf{The bar is a substitution matrix, not zero}
(Section~\ref{sec:trivial}). BLOSUM62 alone reaches 49\% of ESM-2 across 25
assays, a 60-parameter one-hot vector aligns with ESM-2 better than a genomic
model does, and on the genomic models' own benchmark tasks a positional one-hot
wins outright. 194 ProteinGym assays are also 165 independent proteins, and must
be counted as such.
\item \textbf{The transfer does not survive a destroyed map}
(Section~\ref{sec:map}). Frameshifting and reverse-complementing break the
DNA-to-protein map while leaving a plausible genomic string, and a protein-level
representation must lose its signal under both. A representation read as weak
transfer at $\rho=+0.141$ scores $+0.143$ frameshifted. Context dose-response
curves are likewise reproduced by flanks carrying no genomic information, in both
directions of sign (Section~\ref{sec:context}).
\item \textbf{The nulls are nulls, not power failures}
(Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}). Synonymous encodings must share a label by
construction, so the spread of model scores across them bounds the correlation any
proxy built on that model could reach. A single masked pass already prices every
alternative, so this costs nothing. The ceiling is $0.900$: a null at $-0.013$
cannot be blamed on measurement noise.
\item \textbf{What separates Evo~2 is architecture and corpus, not scale}
(Section~\ref{sec:scale}). No Nucleotide Transformer size carries protein-fitness
signal, and three contrasts available from released checkpoints at matched
architecture each leave the result short of the bar.
\end{enumerate}

We report four retractions of our own earlier claims in Section~\ref{sec:retract}.
We do this in the body, not an appendix, because the value of these checks is
precisely that they caught them, and because a reader deciding whether to adopt
them should see what it costs not to have had them.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Protein and genomic language models.} ESM-2 \citep{lin2023esm2} and
ESM-C established masked protein language models as zero-shot variant-effect
predictors, evaluated at scale by ProteinGym \citep{notin2023proteingym}. On the
nucleotide side, the Nucleotide Transformer \citep{dalla2024nucleotide}, HyenaDNA
\citep{nguyen2023hyenadna} and Evo~2 \citep{brixi2025evo2} model genomes directly.
Evo and Evo~2 have been used to design functional sequences
\citep{merchant2025evo,king2026phage}, though in the latter case through
inference-time guidance from separate predictive models and not bare likelihood
ranking.

\paragraph{Genomic models on protein tasks.} \citet{boshar2024glm} ask our question
directly and reach an apparently opposite answer: under supervised fine-tuning on
five protein tasks, Nucleotide Transformer v2 500M matches or outperforms ESM-2 on
three of them. They attribute the two losses to tasks needing codon-level
resolution and train a 3-mer variant to address it, which improves those two tasks
and leaves the rest unchanged. Their protocol differs from ours in the way that
matters: they fine-tune, we score zero-shot likelihood; they use no
non-model baseline, no map-destroying control and no noise ceiling; and they do not
evaluate zero-shot variant effect on deep mutational scans. Section~\ref{sec:tension}
argues that the two results are consistent and that the difference is diagnostic,
since a fine-tuned readout can recover residue identity from nucleotides through the
codon channel of Section~\ref{sec:map} while a likelihood cannot.

\paragraph{Evaluation critiques.} Our position follows a line of work showing that
benchmark gains often survive removal of the mechanism they are credited to
\citep{rendle2019difficulty,musgrave2020metric}. The specific concern that strong
scores reflect data leakage through a known map, and not learned structure, is
familiar from natural language inference artifacts \citep{gururangan2018annotation}.
We contribute the observation that biology supplies an exactly known map and
therefore exactly constructible controls, which natural language does not.

\paragraph{Cross-lingual transfer.} Three of our controls answer questions already
asked of multilingual models, where our construction is unavailable. Whether
measured transfer reflects shared structure or mere lexical overlap was tested by
removing the overlap from training, and the answer was that overlap is not the
mechanism \citep{conneau2020emerging,karthikeyan2020crosslingual}, typological
relatedness mattering more than shared script \citep{pires2019multilingual};
evaluation-side overlap remains a known artifact \citep{artetxe2020translation}.
That test changes the model. Section~\ref{sec:map} holds the model fixed and
destroys the map inside the input while preserving surface statistics, which only
a known map permits. That a language sample is not the independent draw its count
implies is an established concern \citep{ploeger2026principled}, raised against
sampling practice rather than converted into an effective sample size;
Section~\ref{sec:trivial} performs that conversion, at 29 of 194 units. That
label variation is signal and not noise is standard
\citep{plank2022problem,pavlick2019inherent}, yet neither yields a bound on
achievable correlation, which needs inputs sharing a label by construction rather
than by judgement. Section~\ref{sec:ceiling} gets that set free from the genetic
code. We claim no result about language. We claim that three concerns the field
already holds become exactly measurable where the map is known, and we report what
happens when they are measured.

\paragraph{Noise ceilings.} Attenuation corrections are standard in psychometrics
and in neural encoding models, where repeat measurements bound explainable
variance. Our variant obtains the ceiling from model-internal invariance violation
and not from experimental replicates, which makes it available when replicates
are not.

\section{What a Transfer Claim Must Survive}
\label{sec:controls}

Let $f$ be a model scoring inputs in representation $A$, and let a claim assert
that $f$ captures property $P$ defined on modality $B$, related to $A$ by a known
surjective map $\pi: A \to B$. Four checks follow.

\subsection{A noise ceiling from model-internal invariance}
\label{sec:ceiling}

Because $\pi$ is many-to-one, a fibre $\pi^{-1}(b)$ is a set of inputs that must
share the label $P(b)$ exactly. Any variation of $f$ within a fibre is variance
that cannot track the label. Writing $\sigma^2_{\text{within}}$ for the mean
within-fibre variance of $f$ and $\sigma^2_{\text{between}}$ for the variance
across fibres, the correlation between $f$ and any label is attenuated by at most
\begin{equation}
\kappa \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2_{\text{between}}}{\sigma^2_{\text{between}} +
\sigma^2_{\text{within}}}}.
\end{equation}
A true correlation $\rho$ can appear no larger than $\kappa\rho$. Reporting a null
without $\kappa$ leaves open that the measurement had no power.

For genomic models the fibres are synonymous encodings. Crucially this costs
nothing: scoring a variant requires choosing a codon, and the same masked pass
assigns a probability to every synonymous alternative, so the within-fibre spread
is already computed. Across our 25 assays $\kappa = 0.900$, meaning a true
$\rho=0.5$ would still have surfaced at $0.45$.

\subsection{Map-destroying controls}
\label{sec:map}

A claim that $f$'s representation is $B$-level predicts that destroying $\pi$
destroys the signal. The control must destroy $\pi$ while keeping the input inside
$f$'s training distribution, or a drop is confounded with distribution shift.

For DNA two manipulations do this. A frameshift by one nucleotide changes every
downstream codon while leaving the string a real genomic sequence read one base
later. The reverse complement is real sequence on the opposite strand. Both destroy
the protein reading; neither makes the input unnatural.

We stress one negative design lesson. Replacing \emph{all} codons with synonymous
alternatives also preserves the protein, and its signal does drop, but this is a
global distribution shift as much as a map manipulation and is not admissible
evidence. The decisive controls are those that keep the input natural.

\subsection{Composition-matched context twins}
\label{sec:context}

Claims that additional context improves a score require a twin that supplies the
same number of tokens with the same composition and no information. Mononucleotide
and dinucleotide shuffles, applied separately to each flank so that each side's
composition is preserved, give this twin. The reported quantity is the real-minus-
shuffled difference, never the real trend alone.

\subsection{Trivial baselines and correlated units}
\label{sec:trivial}

Every table carries a scorer that uses no learned model. For protein fitness that
is BLOSUM62; for genomic benchmark tasks a positional one-hot or a $k$-mer count;
for representational alignment a one-hot residue encoding. Units are counted at the
level of independence: 194 ProteinGym assays constitute 165 protein clusters, and
our 25 assays constitute 21. Statistics are computed over clusters.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Assays.} Every ProteinGym assay for which a native coding sequence could
be verified, where verification requires that some ENA coding sequence for the
assay's UniProt accession translate to a protein containing the assayed target
exactly. This yields 29 assays. After removing targets longer than 420 aa or with
fewer than 200 usable single mutants, 25 assays across 9 organisms remain: human,
yeast, \textit{E.\ coli}, \textit{B.\ subtilis}, \textit{P.\ aeruginosa},
\textit{K.\ pneumoniae}, \textit{S.\ acidocaldarius}, SARS-CoV-2 and HIV-1.

Only the length cap binds. It removes four assays, at 490, 490, 498 and 498~aa;
the longest target it keeps is 413~aa, so every threshold in $[414, 489]$ selects
the identical panel and the stated value of 420 is not a tuned quantity. The
200-mutant filter removes nothing at all: the smallest surviving assay already
exceeds it. We report both filters because both were declared, not because both
act.

\paragraph{Scoring.} Identical masked-marginal protocol on both modalities, on the
same region: the assayed target sequence for protein models, and the coding
sequence encoding exactly it for genomic models. Only the modality differs. Evo~2
is autoregressive and is scored by summed token log-likelihood.

\paragraph{Models.} ESM-2 650M; Nucleotide Transformer v2 at 50M, 100M, 250M and
500M; HyenaDNA; Evo~2 7B. Genomic model code requires
\texttt{transformers==4.44.2}, since version 5 removes attributes the Nucleotide
Transformer's bundled remote code reads.

\paragraph{Constants, and how they were set.} Table~\ref{tab:hyper} lists every
free quantity below. None was selected by inspecting the outcome it
governs: each was fixed before the contrast it enters was run, and none was
revised afterwards. We state this because the claim here is about validity, and a
check whose own thresholds were tuned against its results would be
self-defeating.


\paragraph{Determinism.} Model scoring is deterministic: masked-marginal and
autoregressive log-likelihoods under fixed weights involve no sampling, so a
re-run reproduces each $\rho$ exactly and a seed sweep over scoring would measure
nothing. The seed enters only the resampling tests, where $20{,}000$ draws leave a
Monte-Carlo error on a $p$ near $0.08$ below $0.004$ --- smaller than the
rounding we report it to. We verified the determinism claim rather than assuming
it: re-running the ESM-2 650M arm on different hardware, a different PyTorch
version and an independently installed dependency set reproduced all 50 published
per-assay values to a maximum absolute difference of $4\times10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Hardware and runtime.} The Nucleotide Transformer arms, including the
2.5B pair, ran on one A100-SXM4-80GB per arm; the 2.5B contrast is the most
expensive in the paper at two arms of 25 assays each. Evo~2 ran once on an NVIDIA
L40S host (\texttt{evo2} 0.6.0). Section~\ref{sec:cost} gives the cost of each
control in model evaluations, which is the portable unit; wall-clock on this
hardware never exceeded a few hours per arm, and no result in the paper requires
more than a single 80GB GPU.

\subsection{Most of the transfer disappears}

\paragraph{The transfer claim does not survive its map control.} A linear probe on
mean-pooled genomic embeddings, trained and evaluated within background groups so
that it must generalise to unseen residues, reaches $\rho = +0.141$. Read alone this
is weak transfer. Table~\ref{tab:map} is the same probe under manipulation.

Frameshifting changes every downstream codon and costs nothing. Reading the
opposite strand, where the sequence is not a coding sequence at all, costs $0.012$.
No protein-level representation can be invariant to either. The mechanism is that
the codon at the varying site already determines the residue, so the probe never
needed a protein-level representation.

A variance decomposition agrees. The fraction of score variance explained by
amino-acid identity is $0.643$ for the genomic likelihood and $0.672$ for the
genomic representation, against $1.000$ for any protein model by construction. The
representation is barely more protein-level than the likelihood.

A 60-parameter one-hot vector shares more with ESM-2 than the genomic model does.

\paragraph{Context trends reproduce from no information.} For the Nucleotide
Transformer, real genomic flanks produce a monotone degradation with trend
$-0.771$; mononucleotide-shuffled flanks reproduce it at $-0.633$ over three seeds.
HyenaDNA with a 30~kb window produces a clean positive trend of $+0.536$ whose
shuffled twins score $+0.893$, $+0.750$ and $+0.750$, so real minus shuffled is
$-0.008$ and real is higher in 1 of 6 comparisons. Both signs are reproduced by
flanks that carry nothing.

For Evo~2 the control is different and sharper. No arm of its context curve carries
specificity signal at all: the bare coding sequence scores AUC $0.401$ with
interval $[0.345,0.456]$, significantly \emph{below} chance, and every flanked arm
lies between $0.521$ and $0.543$ with an interval containing $0.5$. The reported
$+0.70$ trend over five points carries an exact permutation $p$ of $0.117$, falls
to $+0.400$ with $p=0.375$ once the no-context point is dropped, and the 300~nt and
5400~nt score vectors correlate at $+0.963$. An eighteen-fold increase in context
past the first increment changes AUC by $+0.016$ and then nothing.

\subsection{Transfer across 25 assays}
\label{sec:assays}

Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(a) is the headline. Three readings follow, and the trivial
baseline decides all three.

A substitution matrix with no model of any kind reaches 49\% of ESM-2 and is
positive on 24 of 25 assays. Five fitted chemistry scalars with weights learned on
other protein clusters reach 61\%. ESM-2's genuinely learned margin over fitted
chemistry is $+0.179$, not $+0.465$. The bar a genomic model must clear is
therefore $+0.228$, not $+0.465$.

The Nucleotide Transformer does not clear it, losing to BLOSUM62 by $+0.241$
$[+0.200,+0.283]$ in 24 of 25 assays. Its attenuation ceiling is $0.900$, so this
is a null and not a power failure.

\paragraph{The marginalised score, specified.} Summing the codon fibre out removes
the leak of Section~\ref{sec:map} by construction and is the only genomic score
entitled to be called a protein-level proxy. It scores $-0.008$, and averaging
log-ratios over the fibre instead of log-summing them --- a geometric mean of
codon likelihoods, not a marginal --- gives $-0.019$. Both are nulls whose
intervals contain zero, so nothing in Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(a) turns on the
choice; Appendix~\ref{sec:codon} reports the $+0.011$ gap in full, because it is
wider than a corpus contrast we went to the trouble of measuring.

Evo~2 does clear zero, at $+0.266$ with an interval excluding it and positive on 23
of 25 assays. It does not clear the substitution matrix: Evo~2 minus BLOSUM62 is
$+0.0376$ with interval $[-0.012,+0.087]$, higher in 14 of 25 assays and lower in
11 (Figure~\ref{fig:spread}(a)). Against the fitted
chemistry ridge it is behind, at $-0.0202$.

\subsection{Scale is not the explanation}
\label{sec:scale}

The Nucleotide Transformer is flat in level across four sizes but not in order,
and the distinction decides what the extrapolation below is worth. The four means
run from $-0.0132$ at 50M to $+0.0083$ at 500M and every one of their intervals
contains zero, so no size in the family carries protein-fitness signal. They are
nonetheless monotone in parameter count, Spearman $+1.000$. That ordering belongs
to this score and not to the family: under either marginalisation of the preceding
paragraph the 100M rung falls below the 50M one and the coefficient drops to
$+0.800$. The trend we fit below is therefore the most favourable of the three
orderings available, which is the spirit in which it is offered.

Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(b) shows the ladder. We fit a trend to
grant the scaling account its best case, not because these data
establish one. Regressing $\rho$ on $\log_{10} N$ gives a slope of $+0.0207$ per
decade and an intercept of $-0.1762$; on four points that slope carries a standard
error of $0.0069$ and is not significant, at $p=0.083$ by exact permutation over
all $24$ orderings of the four means, where the attainable floor is $2/24=0.083$. Extrapolated, the trend
predicts $+0.0281$ at 7B parameters against Evo~2's measured $+0.2658$, short by a
factor of $9.5$, and reaching BLOSUM62 along it would require $10^{19.5}$
parameters. That second figure is what happens when a trend fitted inside the noise
is extended eleven orders of magnitude, and is quoted to show the absurdity rather
than as an estimate. The first is better read as a lower bound on the gap: even on
the most generous reading of this family's trajectory, scale does not reach Evo~2.

Evo~2 therefore sits far off the line that scaling its family would trace. Whatever
produces its protein-fitness signal is a property of architecture and corpus, and
scale alone does not buy it. We note the limitation squarely: with one point off
the line we can reject the scaling account but cannot identify which of
architecture, corpus or token budget is responsible, because none of these is a
variable we control. The Limitations section says what would.

\subsection{The nulls are not contamination}
\label{sec:membership}

An audit of an earlier draft objected that the assay coding sequences are
database-canonical genes, plausibly verbatim substrings of NT-v2's 850-genome
corpus, so a gene and its rotations would both be training text and the
reading-frame null of Section~\ref{sec:map} would follow from memorisation alone.
That corpus is a known finite set of genomes, so membership can be checked rather
than argued. We reconstruct masked spans in 27{,}787 trials over 720 windows of 3{,}000~nt,
drawn from 11 genomes verified present in the corpus and 13 released after its
training finished, with the window pools matched on GC in 1\% bins to $0.5763$
against $0.5768$. Member genomes are reconstructed no better than non-members:
per-nucleotide accuracy differs by $-0.0077$ ($p=0.647$), with the point estimate
running the wrong way. The sensitive attack agrees. Separating member from
non-member windows by mean $\log p(\text{true})$ gives AUC between $0.494$ and
$0.540$ across six span lengths, which is chance. Exact span recovery is zero
beyond a single token in every arm. The chromosome used throughout this paper is
not a corpus member. Memorisation is not what produced the nulls.

At 500M the same attack appears to succeed, at AUC $0.664$, and the apparent
signal lives entirely in a comparison whose GC matching failed by five points:
Appendix~\ref{sec:mem500} has it. We do not claim memorisation is excluded at
500M, only that there is no composition-free evidence for it.

\subsection{Architecture and corpus, measured from released checkpoints}
\label{sec:stage0}

Section~\ref{sec:scale} rejects the scaling account but cannot say which of
architecture, corpus or token budget produces Evo~2's signal, because those are
confounded between the two families. Released checkpoints resolve three contrasts at
matched architecture, for inference cost only. We ran all three. The token budget is
not among them, and the Limitations section says why no number of
released checkpoints can supply it.

Each pair below is identical in every dimension but one; Table~\ref{tab:hyper}
gives the dimensions.

\paragraph{Codon-aligned tokenization does not help.} A 3-mer token is exactly a
codon, so the masked token \emph{is} the codon under test and the
neighbouring-codon conditioning the 6-mer scorer carries disappears.
\citet{boshar2024glm} trained this variant because codon-level resolution was where
genomic models lost to protein models under fine-tuning, and reported that it
improved their two codon-sensitive tasks. It had not been scored zero-shot.

\paragraph{Within-species corpus diversity does not help.} \texttt{500m-human-ref}
against \texttt{500m-1000g}: one human reference genome against 3,202 genetically
diverse ones.

\paragraph{Cross-species corpus breadth does not help either.}
\texttt{2.5b-1000g} against \texttt{2.5b-multi-species}: human against 850 species.
This is the most informative of the three, because Evo~2 trains across all domains
of life and cross-species range was the most plausible surviving account of its
margin. It is also the contrast on which our own conclusion was staked: an earlier
version of this paper reported it as unrun and said explicitly that a gradient here
would change the recommendation we now make in the Limitations section.

Figure~\ref{fig:panels}(c) reports all three. No factor moves the result past the bar.
Codon-aligned tokenization, 3-mer minus 6-mer, gives $-0.0156$ $[-0.043,+0.012]$,
and three thousand genomes of within-species variation, 1000G minus human-ref, give
$-0.0054$ $[-0.025,+0.013]$. Both contain zero, and neither point estimate licenses
the claim that the richer option is worse. Every attenuation ceiling lies between
$0.846$ and $0.901$, so none of these nulls is a power failure, with one cell
excepted: \texttt{AMIE\_PSEAE\_Wrenbeck\_2017} under the 850-species model returns
$\kappa=0$, its synonymous spread exactly swamping its between-variant spread, and we
report that cell as uninformative rather than null. Excluding it moves the breadth
difference from $+0.0395$ to $+0.0397$ at the cluster level.

\paragraph{Breadth, and the one contrast where our two estimators disagree.}
Cross-species breadth is larger than either of the other two factors and is the only
contrast in this paper whose verdict depends on which interval is used. The
percentile bootstrap over assays, the estimator used for every other row of
Table~\ref{tab:free}, gives $+0.0322$ $[+0.001,+0.068]$ and excludes zero by
$0.0011$. The $t$ interval with a sign-flip permutation test, which is what our
released module implements and what we report at both unit levels throughout, gives
the identical point estimate with $[-0.004,+0.069]$ and $p=0.079$, containing zero;
at the cluster level it gives $+0.0395$ $[-0.003,+0.082]$ with $p=0.066$.

We report the disagreement rather than choosing the convenient side of it, and we
take the $t$ interval as governing, for the reason that motivates this paper: a
percentile bound sitting $0.0011$ from zero at $n=25$ is not a measurement of a
gradient, and the minimum detectable effect of the cluster-level test is $0.0573$,
above the $+0.0395$ observed. This is a non-detection on an underpowered panel, not
a demonstration that breadth buys nothing, and we say so in both directions: a larger
panel could resolve it, and we would not be surprised if breadth were a small real
effect. What it is not is a gradient that reaches the bar. At $+0.0395$ against a
$0.20$ gap to BLOSUM62, a confirmed breadth effect of this size would leave the
850-species model carrying no transfer, so it would sharpen the account of Evo~2's
margin without changing any claim in this paper.

The honesty cost of this is worth stating plainly. An earlier version of this paper
named breadth as the contrast whose outcome would change our recommendation. Had we
read only the bootstrap column, we would now be reporting that it showed a gradient
and reopening a compute argument on the strength of $0.0011$.

\section{Four Claims That Were False}
\label{sec:retract}

\textbf{A reading-frame result was retracted.} A genomic model appeared unable to
distinguish a gene from the same gene read one nucleotide out of frame. The
``frameshift'' is a rotation, so apart from one wrap junction it is real genomic
sequence. Ranking the four conditions by whether they yield a real genomic string
reproduces the entire table with no appeal to reading frame. The replacement probe,
calibrated against a 3-periodic Markov positive control, is underpowered and not
null: bounding it needs about 214 unique coding sequences and 24 are available.

\textbf{A representation-level transfer claim was falsified by its own control},
as Table~\ref{tab:map} shows.

\textbf{A context dose-response was measuring composition}, in both directions.

\textbf{A general claim was too general.} ``A genomic model carries no
protein-fitness signal'' held for the Nucleotide Transformer family and was written
as though it held for genomic models. Evo~2 refutes it.

\section{The Checks Cost Less Than the Experiments}
\label{sec:cost}

The noise ceiling is read off a masked pass already performed, trivial baselines
and cluster-level statistics are free, and each remaining check costs one extra
scoring pass. The Evo~2 context control, the most expensive of them, is 41.9M
tokens against 167M for the uncontrolled arms it corrects. A control cheaper
than the experiment it checks has no compute excuse.
Appendix~\ref{sec:costdetail} itemises them.

\section{Conclusion}

Most of the reported genomic-to-protein transfer is not transfer. A probe on
genomic embeddings scores as well on sequence whose reading frame has been
destroyed as on real genes; the Nucleotide Transformer family is null at every
size we could run, and its one monotone trend belongs to a scoring convention
rather than to the family; a context dose-response was composition in both
directions. What survives is a single positive result that a substitution matrix
already matches: Evo~2 transfers at $+0.266$ against BLOSUM62's $+0.228$, and the
paired gap of $+0.0376$ has an interval containing zero. Four claims fell, three of them ours, and none of
the three factors we can vary at matched architecture explains what is left.

Nothing in the four checks is specific to biology. Any claim that a
representation encodes a property of another modality related to it by a known
map can in principle be checked by destroying the map, by bounding attenuation
from within-fibre variance, and by matching a context manipulation on
composition --- in principle, because we have tested this in one domain, and the
hard step outside it is constructing a map-destroying manipulation when the map
is not exact. Biology is where the map is known exactly, which makes it the
setting where these checks are exactly constructible and their consequences
visible.

A reviewer entitled to ask what would change our mind had a short answer in an
earlier version of this paper: cross-species corpus breadth, at the cost of one
download. We ran it, and it did not change our mind, which is the outcome we had
committed in advance to reporting either way. The answer is now longer. The
token budget would change our mind, and no number of released checkpoints can
supply it.

The most interesting thing we learned from that last contrast was not its value.
It was that two standard interval estimators disagreed on it, one excluding zero
by $0.0011$ and one not, and that reporting both is what made the
disagreement visible rather than decisive. A paper about measurement validity
should be able to report that its own last experiment came within $0.0011$ of a
different conclusion.

\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{One benchmark.} Every measurement here is on ProteinGym, and the
25-assay panel is a subset of it chosen by coding-sequence verifiability rather
than at random. The controls are therefore demonstrated on one dataset. Two
consequences are worth separating. The \emph{negative} results are the more
robust of the two, because a control that removes a signal on this panel would
have to be rescued by another panel to be wrong, and we report the attenuation
ceiling that rules out the panel being too noisy to read. The \emph{null} results
are the more fragile: a non-detection is a statement about this panel's power, and
we report the minimum detectable effect with every one rather than calling it
equivalence. What one benchmark cannot establish is that the effect sizes
transfer. A different panel --- different proteins, different assay types,
different depth --- could move every number in Section~\ref{sec:scale} while
leaving these verdicts intact, and nothing here tests that.

\paragraph{Generality is argued, not shown.} The claim that these controls port
outside biology rests on each one's precondition being statable without reference
to biology, not on any non-biological experiment. Biology is the easy case in a
specific way: the map from DNA to protein is known exactly and is the same
everywhere, so the map-destroying control is exactly constructible. In a setting
where the map is approximate or learned --- an image--caption encoder, a
code--language model --- destroying it is itself a modelling problem, and a
manipulation that is only approximately map-destroying may leave exactly the
leakage it was built to detect. We expect the ceiling and the trivial-baseline
controls to port most readily, since both need only a construct-shared label set,
and the map control to port least readily. Testing that ordering is future work we
have not done.

\paragraph{Models and runs.} Evo~2 numbers come from a single run on one GPU host and describe \texttt{evo2\_7b},
since \texttt{evo2\_1b\_base} requires Transformer Engine. We have one genomic
architecture off the Nucleotide Transformer scaling line and therefore cannot
separate architecture from corpus from token budget.

Public releases fix the \emph{token budget} at one value per model: we verified
that neither the Nucleotide Transformer nor Evo~2 publishes intermediate-step
checkpoints, every repository carrying a single branch and one weight file. The
token axis is therefore closed to any study of frozen models.

Corpus and tokenization are not closed, and Section~\ref{sec:stage0} measures all
three contrasts they afford. None moves the result past the bar, which bears on what
a pretraining ladder could be expected to find. Of the three factors separable
without training a model, all three are flat.

What remains genuinely unobservable from released models is the token budget and
any factor crossed with it. A ladder pretrained under controlled variation of
parameters, tokens and corpus composition would close it, and Appendix~A gives a
design and budget. We state the case against that ladder as plainly as the case
for it, and with breadth now measured the case against has got stronger rather than
weaker: all three free axes are flat, so the argument for the spend rests on the
token axis alone --- a single unmeasured factor carrying a five-figure compute
request. We do not undertake it, and we think the honest form of this contribution is
the measurement with three controlled negative contrasts behind it rather
than a trained ladder expected to find the same thing.

That recommendation is now made on a measurement rather than on a projection, which
is the only reason we are willing to state it this firmly. We flag the one way it
could still be wrong: breadth is the largest of the three contrasts, its cluster-level
test could not have detected an effect below $0.0573$, and a panel larger than 25
assays could turn it into a small real effect. That would locate part of Evo~2's
margin in corpus range, which is a result we would welcome, and it would still leave
the 850-species model $0.20$ short of a substitution matrix.

Every assay used here has homologs, so the regime where a sequence has no relatives
is untested. The regulatory arm covers two small models and licenses nothing about
larger ones. The specificity arm rests on one combinatorially complete landscape.

\section{The 500M Membership Arm}
\label{sec:mem500}

At 500M the membership attack of Section~\ref{sec:membership} returns AUC $0.664$
rather than the $0.494$ to $0.540$ it returns at 50M. Its two pools differ by five
points of GC, $0.603$ against $0.553$, despite carrying the matched label in the
analysis output, and genomic models are strongly composition-sensitive. The
genus-matched comparison, where composition does hold, returns AUC $0.462$ and
per-nucleotide differences of $-0.0097$ and $+0.0028$: the member genome is no
better than its non-member relatives. A signal that appears only in the comparison
whose matching failed, and vanishes where it holds, is the artifact of
Sections~\ref{sec:map} and~\ref{sec:context} a fourth time, found here in a control
of our own rather than in a published claim. It is not evidence of memorisation,
and it does not exclude it either: the matched comparison rests on a single member
genome and 40 windows per arm, which is too thin to rule out a moderate effect.
Closing the question needs a properly matched pool at 500M, not a larger model.

\section{The Cost of Each Check}
\label{sec:costdetail}

The noise ceiling is free: it is read off a masked pass already performed. The map
controls cost one additional scoring pass per manipulation, so two passes over the
evaluation set. Composition-matched twins cost one pass per shuffle seed per
condition; we use two seeds and two shuffle orders. Trivial baselines cost
negligible compute. Cluster-level statistics cost nothing but a join.

For the Evo~2 context control specifically, scoring the 399-variant discrimination
subset instead of all 7{,}882 variants is a twentyfold saving that changes no
reported number, because the published AUCs are read off that subset already. The
full control is 21 arms, 8{,}379 sequences and 41.9M tokens, against 167M for the
original uncontrolled arms.

\section{Genomic Models on Their Own Benchmark Tasks}
\label{app:owntask}
Grading genomic models on coding sequence and protein fitness is grading them on a
task they were not built for. On five Nucleotide Transformer benchmark tasks under
a homology-clustered split, with frozen embeddings and a linear probe, the trivial
competitor wins outright twice and loses twice by under $0.03$ AUC. A positional
one-hot reaches $0.959$ on splice sites against the Nucleotide Transformer's
$0.756$ and HyenaDNA's $0.704$, a loss of $0.203$ and $0.254$. The one-hot
competitor is selected on test, so the baseline is given an oracle the model is
not, and still wins.

Concurrent work reaches this result independently and in a stronger form.
\citet{cheng2026invariance} evaluate five genomic models, including Evo2-1B, on a
650-sequence benchmark with scrambled controls, and report universal
strand-blindness and a 100-parameter position-aware PWM at perfect performance
where billion-parameter models fail; they further find that larger models show
\emph{stronger} compositional bias, so scale amplifies the failure rather than
correcting it. Their benchmark is purpose-built and better powered than our
five-task probe. We therefore present this subsection as a corroborated side
result rather than a contribution, and note that it is the arm of this work that
claims least: the controls and the zero-shot transfer measurement are where our
contribution sits.


\section{Reconciling Fine-Tuned and Zero-Shot Results}
\label{sec:tension}
\citet{boshar2024glm} report Nucleotide Transformer v2 500M matching or beating
ESM-2 on three of five protein tasks. We report the same family at $\rho=-0.013$
against ESM-2's $+0.465$. Both can hold, and the map control says why.

Fine-tuning fits a readout on top of the representation. Section~\ref{sec:map}
shows that the genomic representation carries residue identity through the codon,
and that this survives frameshifting and reverse-complementing. A supervised head
is free to use that channel, so strong fine-tuned performance on a protein task is
evidence that residue identity is recoverable and not that the model represents
protein-level structure. A zero-shot likelihood has no fitted head and therefore no
access to the channel, which is why the same model scores at chance.

This makes the two measurements complementary and gives a testable prediction: a
model whose fine-tuned protein performance comes from the codon channel should show
that performance survive a frameshift of its input. We did not run this and regard
it as the sharpest available test of the account, since it requires only re-running
an existing fine-tuning pipeline on frameshifted sequences.

A codon-aligned tokenization is the natural place to look for a genuine effect, and
one exists at matched architecture: \texttt{nucleotide-transformer-v2-50m-3mer-}
\texttt{multi-species} differs from its 6-mer counterpart only in vocabulary size,
75 against 4107, with identical depth, width, head count and training corpus. We
report it zero-shot in Table~\ref{tab:free} as the one architectural change prior
work identified as promising for protein tasks.


\section{Interval Width at Partial Sample Size}
\label{app:sweep}
We note one further sampling lesson from the runs themselves. At 14 of the 25 assays
the tokenizer difference read $-0.0302$ with an interval excluding zero, and that did
not survive the remaining eleven. These intervals are wide at small $n$ and a partial
sweep is not a result --- which is the same lesson the breadth bootstrap teaches at
full $n$, one estimator removed.
